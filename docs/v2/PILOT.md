**Mechanism.** `asyncio.timeout` cancels the await, not the worker thread. `_Session.finish` writes the outcome at the cut, then `asyncio.run` joins the executor for at most 300 s (Python 3.12 `THREAD_JOIN_TIMEOUT`), and only after that does `investigation/runner.py` read `lab.fits_used`, `lab.world.experiments_run`, `lab.submitted` and `lab.prediction_outcomes`. So anything an in-flight tool does inside that window reaches the cell and not the record. All three sessions have a `fit` as their last unanswered tool_use.

- dev-012 s1: the driver's wall is 268 s past the record, under the join limit, and the run log has no join warning. The fit landed before the read: reading 35, record 34.
- dev-005 s1 and dev-008 s1: 1,455 and 1,034 s past the record. `.cache/pilot-llm-run.log` has `The executor did not finishing joining its threads within 300 seconds` immediately before each of these two session lines (lines 61 and 99), and nowhere else. The state was read while the fit still ran, so their `fits_used` agrees with the record because of timing, not because the read is safe. The thread then held the process until the fit ended: `process.join()` after the outcome arrives has no limit, so both ran past the 4,200 s hard-timeout deadline without being killed.

**One correction to the reading in the hand-over.** dev-012's record has two unanswered `fit` tool_uses, but only one reached lab state. Had two run, the join (finished inside 300 s) would have waited for both and the reading would be 36, unless one raised. Both fits share one message id, and neither repeats an earlier fit on the same data [red team, `rv_fin.py`]. That the second never committed is an inference from the join; the store cannot show dispatch order, nor which of the two landed.
